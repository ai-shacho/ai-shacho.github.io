Why Are My Cold Emails Going to Spam in 2026?

If your open rates suddenly cratered — or one provider (say, every Gmail recipient) just stopped responding — it's almost never your copy. It's deliverability. Here's exactly why, and how to fix it.

In 2024, Google and Yahoo began requiring bulk senders to authenticate their mail, keep complaints low, and offer one-click unsubscribe. In 2025, Microsoft / Outlook started routing — and increasingly rejecting — high-volume mail that isn't authenticated, and Yahoo shifted from judging your IP to judging your domain. The old playbook ("warm up an inbox and blast") quietly stopped working. If your setup isn't right, even a perfectly written email never reaches a human.

The 5 most common reasons (in order)

1. You're missing SPF, DKIM or DMARC

These three DNS records prove your mail is really from you:

  • SPF — lists which servers may send for your domain.
  • DKIM — cryptographically signs each message.
  • DMARC — tells inboxes what to do when the first two fail.

For bulk senders, all three are now effectively mandatory. Send a test to a Gmail address you own, open it, choose Show original, and confirm you see SPF: PASS, DKIM: PASS and DMARC: PASS. If any says FAIL or NONE, that's your problem.

2. You ramped volume too fast

A brand-new domain that sends 500 emails on day one looks exactly like a spammer. New domains should start at ~20/day and ramp over 2–4 weeks. If spam started after you scaled, this is almost certainly it — drop back to your last safe volume and ramp slower.

3. You're sending from your main domain

Cold outreach should go from a separate domain or subdomain (e.g. mail.yourco.com). That way a reputation hit doesn't poison the domain you use for real business email.

4. Your content trips filters

  • Heavy HTML, multiple links, or big images on a first touch.
  • Spam-trigger phrasing ("FREE!!!", "act now", "100% guaranteed").
  • No clear unsubscribe / opt-out.

First-touch cold emails should be plain text, one link maximum (ideally none), and short.

5. Your list is dirty

High bounce rates wreck your reputation fast. Verify your list before sending and remove invalid, role-based (info@, sales@) and risky addresses.

The fix, in order

  1. Authenticate: publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC, then verify all three PASS.
  2. Use a dedicated sending domain.
  3. Warm up and keep daily volume sane for your domain age.
  4. Keep complaints under 0.1% and honour opt-outs.
  5. Run a pre-send check before every campaign.

Cold Email Templates for Recruiters (2026)

Recruiting runs on two kinds of cold email: getting passive candidates to reply, and getting companies to hand you roles to fill. Here are templates that work for both — plus the one rule most recruiters get wrong.

The rule that changes everything (candidates)

Don't pitch the job first. Passive candidates ignore "I have a role for you" messages because they get dozens. Earn interest first — compliment something specific about their work and ask if they're open. Reveal the role only once they bite.

Candidate — first touch

Subject: {name}, your work on {project} caught my eye Hi {name} — I came across your work on {specific thing} and was genuinely impressed by {detail}. I work with {company_type} teams and occasionally come across roles that might be a step up for someone with your background. No pitch today — just wondering if you're open to hearing about the right one if it comes up?

Candidate — follow-up (add a new detail)

Subject: one more thing, {name} Quick follow-up — the role I mentioned is fully remote and the team's shipping {impactful thing}. Happy to share specifics if it's interesting; if not, no worries at all.

The rule that changes everything (clients)

Lead with risk reversal. Companies get pitched by agencies constantly. Remove the "what if it doesn't work?" fear up front: pay-on-hire, a replacement guarantee, or a free trial role.

Client — company that's hiring

Subject: 2 candidates for your {role} opening Hi {name} — saw {company} is hiring for {role}. That's a tough one to fill right now. I specialize in {niche} and could send 2–3 pre-vetted candidates this week — you only pay if you hire. Worth a quick look?

Client — just-funded company

Subject: congrats on the {round} — staffing up? Hi {name} — congrats on the {round}! Scaling usually means hiring pressure fast. I place {roles} on a pay-on-hire basis with a {guarantee} replacement guarantee. Want me to line up a couple of candidates so you can see the quality risk-free?

What makes recruiter cold email work in 2026

  • Under ~60 words, plain text, one clear ask.
  • Signal-based, not generic: reference a funding round, a job post, a recent project. Signal-based outreach replies far better than spray-and-pray.
  • Every follow-up adds a new detail — never "just bumping this."
  • Deliverability first: if your domain isn't authenticated (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), even great templates hit spam.

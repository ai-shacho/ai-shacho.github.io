SPF, DKIM & DMARC Setup for Cold Email (2026)

Since Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft tightened their sender rules, these three DNS records decide whether your cold email reaches the inbox or gets rejected. Here's how to set up all three — step by step, in plain English.

You don't need to be technical. Each record is a single line you add at whoever manages your domain's DNS (Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Namecheap, Google Domains, etc.). Changes can take up to ~24 hours to propagate.

What each record does

  • SPF — lists which servers are allowed to send email for your domain.
  • DKIM — adds a cryptographic signature proving the message really came from you and wasn't altered.
  • DMARC — tells receiving inboxes what to do when SPF or DKIM fail, and where to send reports.
All three are now effectively mandatory for anyone sending at volume. Miss one and Gmail/Yahoo may spam-folder you; Microsoft increasingly rejects unauthenticated mail outright.

Step 1 — SPF

You may only have one SPF record, so merge all your senders into it. Common values:

Google Workspace:  v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
Microsoft 365:     v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all
SendGrid:          v=spf1 include:sendgrid.net ~all

If you send through more than one service, combine the includes into a single record, e.g. v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:sendgrid.net ~all.

Step 2 — DKIM

Google Workspace

Admin console → Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → Authenticate email → generate the key → publish the TXT record it gives you → turn on signing.

Microsoft 365

Microsoft 365 Defender → Policies → DKIM → enable for your domain → publish the two CNAME records shown.

Custom / SMTP provider

Get the exact DKIM CNAME/TXT records from your provider's docs (SendGrid, Postmark, Amazon SES, etc.) and add them as given.

Step 3 — DMARC

Add one TXT record at _dmarc.yourdomain.com. Start in monitor mode:

v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@yourdomain.com

Watch the reports for a couple of weeks, confirm your legitimate mail passes, then tighten:

p=none  →  p=quarantine  →  p=reject
Don't jump straight to p=reject. Monitor first, or you risk blocking your own mail.

Step 4 — Verify it actually works

  1. Send a test to a Gmail address you own. Open it → ⋮ → Show original. Confirm SPF: PASS, DKIM: PASS, DMARC: PASS.
  2. Run your domain through a public lookup (search "MXToolbox" or "dmarcian inspector").
  3. Send through a free inbox-placement checker (search "mail-tester") and aim for 9–10/10.

Cold Email Warm-Up: How Long and How Much (2026)

Send too much, too soon from a fresh domain and you look exactly like a spammer. Warm-up is how you build a sending reputation gradually so your mail keeps reaching the inbox. Here's how long it takes and what volumes are safe.

What "warm-up" actually means

Warm-up is sending a slowly increasing volume of email over a few weeks so that Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft learn your domain is a legitimate, consistent sender — not a burst-and-vanish spammer. Reputation in 2026 is judged largely at the domain level, so this matters even if you change IPs.

How long does it take?

  • Brand-new domain (< 3 weeks): 2–4 weeks of ramping before sending at full volume.
  • A few months old: ~1–2 weeks to scale up safely.
  • Established (1yr+): you can ramp faster, but still watch complaints.

A safe ramp schedule (new domain)

WeekApprox. emails/day
Week 1~20
Week 2~30–40
Week 3~60–80
Week 4~120–150
Week 5+ramp to target, then hold steady
These are guidelines, not guarantees. Watch your numbers: if spam complaints rise above 0.1% or bounces spike, slow down — don't push through it.

Warm-up mistakes that get you flagged

  • Blasting hundreds on day one.
  • Sending before SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass.
  • Inconsistent volume (0 for days, then a huge spike).
  • Sending to a stale list with high bounces during warm-up.
  • Heavy HTML / multiple links on early sends.

Warm-up checklist

  1. Authenticate first (SPF/DKIM/DMARC all PASS).
  2. Start low, ramp gradually, send consistently each day.
  3. Keep early emails plain text with one link max.
  4. Use a clean, verified list.
  5. Monitor complaints and bounces; back off if they rise.
